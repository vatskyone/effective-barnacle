# WEB-1 Guide

This is the full manual for WEB-1. In the app itself, press the **HELP** key (**?**, or **-** on a computer) at any time to see what every key does on the screen you are on; **Shift + HELP** or the **guide** button under the device opens a short controls card. New to WEB-1? Read [Start here](#start-here) first.

## Start here

- **The demo song.** The first time you open WEB-1, it makes a demo song with its own sounds and puts it on the four tape tracks, in a project called **DEMO SONG**. Press **PLAY** to hear it: drums on track 1, bass on 2, pads on 3 and a lead on 4, in A minor at 108 BPM, with section markers for the INTRO, VERSE, CHORUS and OUTRO. Mute tracks in the mixer, jump between the sections (Shift + LOOP in tape mode), play along on the keys (they are set to A minor), or try the live effects on it. **PROJECTS → DEMO SONG** (Shift + 7 in tape mode), or **SYSTEM → ABOUT → DEMO SONG**, makes it again in a new project. Your other projects are kept.
- **The guided tour.** On first launch a short tour starts too: eight steps at the bottom of the screen, each pointing at the key or knob to use (it blinks orange and gets an orange outline), and moving on once you use it. On a computer, each step also shows the keyboard key in brackets. **Shift + STOP** ends it; **SYSTEM → ABOUT → GUIDED TOUR** (Shift + 8 in tape mode) starts it again.

  | Step | What you do | What it shows |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Press **PLAY** (Space) | The demo song plays from the tape |
  | 2 | Press **MIXER** (V) | The four tracks, one colour each |
  | 3 | Turn the **blue knob** | The four knobs change the four coloured values: here, track 1's level |
  | 4 | Press **SYNTH** (Z), then play a few keys | The keyboard, set to the song's key |
  | 5 | Press **T2** (N) | T1–T4 pick the page; 1–8 and Shift + 1–8 pick the sound |
  | 6 | Press **TAPE** (C) | T1–T4 pick the track, REC records on it |
  | 7 | Press **FX** (I), then hold **2** | The master effects, and a live beat repeat on the song |
  | 8 | Any key | Where to find help, and how to start your own song |
- **Tips.** The first few times you open a screen, a one-line tip shows at the bottom of the screen for a few seconds. Turn them off, or on again to see them all again, in **SYSTEM → DISPLAY → TIPS**.
- **The HELP key (?).** On any screen, tap **HELP** (the **-** key) to see what every key does there, in two columns: **KEYS** and **SHIFT +**. It stays up until you press another key, which then does its job. Hold HELP instead to peek, and it closes when you let go. **Shift + HELP** opens the full controls card.

## 1. The device at a glance

- **Screen** (top left, set flush into the case with no frame): shows the current mode and an animated picture of the sound. It fills its whole tile: the title runs along the top edge (where you are in grey, the page you are on in white, for example SYNTH A1 · CLUSTER), the picture sits in the middle, and the knob bar along the bottom edge shows the four knobs: each knob's name small, with its value large underneath in the knob's colour. The tape screen leaves them out to make room for the tape. It is always a black display, whichever finish you choose.
- **Four coloured knobs** (blue, green, white, orange), each sitting in a ring with a short pointer near its edge: each changes the value of the same colour on the screen. Drag up or down or scroll over a knob to turn it. Double-click (double-tap on a phone) **pushes** the knob: it resets the value to its default (the value the loaded preset set, or the factory value), or does the knob's action, such as SAVE. The screen confirms it, for example CUTOFF RESET. See [Knob clicks and pushes](#knob-clicks-and-pushes).
- **Volume knob** (small white knob, top left): the master output level.
- **HELP key (?)** (below the volume knob, the **-** key): shows what every key does on the screen you are on. Shift + HELP opens the controls card.
- **TEMPO key** (right edge, between FX and SEQ, the **`** key): opens the tempo screen, and blinks on every beat while the metronome is on. Shift + TEMPO opens KEY & CHORDS.
- **Mode keys:** SYNTH, DRUM, TAPE and MIXER. SYNTH lights orange while the synths & presets menu is open (Shift + SYNTH), DRUM while the drum machine is open (Shift + DRUM).
- **T1–T4:** pick the page inside a mode.
- **1–8:** sound slots. They choose a synth, or a kit in drum mode. With bank B selected, the slot light turns orange. Keys 1–3 are labelled with the tape tricks, and key 4 with SAMPLE, the sampler (Shift + 4). All eight numbers sit at the same height in their rings, labelled or not.
- **Transport:** REC (a red disc inside the key's ring, Shift + REC for advanced recording), PLAY and STOP, plus the ← → arrows: pitch bend in synth mode, tape winding in the other modes.
- **Musical keys:** the keyboard along the bottom, with white and black keys.
- **Lights on the right edge:** the output level meter. The red light at the top means you are near the maximum level.
- **Speaker grille** (top left corner, a round grille of dots): decoration only; the sound comes from your computer.
- **FX key:** its four dots show which master effects are on, in either bank; it lights orange while FX bank B is on screen.

All the controls sit in one block, separated by thin black lines, so each key reads as its own button. Each key has a large ring with a bold icon or number. In the black keys' row, the outer key of each group widens out to the next white-key line, so the row has no gaps, while each round black cap stays centred over its note. The row sits on the same black plate as everything else, so the rounded corners of the black keys meet clean black lines with no light specks. The WEB-1 name and the "by Snow Bayles - 2026" engraving run up the right edge of the case.

Everything on the device can be clicked or tapped, and almost everything also has a computer-keyboard shortcut. The controls card opens from the **guide** button under the device, or with **Shift + HELP** (Shift + -). HELP on its own shows what the keys do on the current screen.

### Knob clicks and pushes

On the WEB-1 hardware the knobs click as they turn, and **each click is one step**: one item in a menu or list, one letter, one choice such as a key or a scale, one beat of the tape. Values such as levels move by the smallest step the screen shows, and speed up when you turn quickly. Pressing a knob **pushes** it. On a computer, double-click the knob to push it (double-tap on a phone).

A push does the action shown on that knob, or puts the value back where it was before you turned it:

| Where | Knob | Push |
|---|---|---|
| Save preset (Shift + T1) | White: SAVE | Saves the preset |
| | Orange: DELETE | Push twice to delete: the first push shows PUSH AGAIN, and the second must come within 3 seconds |
| Projects (Shift + 7) | White: DO IT | Does the chosen action. DELETE takes two pushes |
| System menu (Shift + 8) | White: SET · CONFIRM · OPEN | Steps the setting, runs the item, or opens it. The ERASE items, POWER OFF and INSTALL UPDATE take two pushes |
| Mixer bank B, T3 | White: BOUNCE | Bounces the chosen tracks |
| Mixer bank B, T4 | Green: GO | Exports, or starts and stops a live mixdown |
| USB stick list | White: LOAD | Loads the file |
| Everywhere else | Any knob | Resets the value to its default: the loaded preset's value, or the factory value |

Turning a knob all the way to YES still works everywhere. On the hardware, the screen's hints say PUSH WHITE instead of TURN WHITE.

## 3. Synth mode

Press **SYNTH** (Z). The T keys choose the page:

| Page | Knobs |
|---|---|
| **T1 ENGINE** | The engine's own four parameters (listed below). Hold Shift for the **MACRO** (see below). Press T1 again for the **SAMPLER** (section 5) |
| **T2 ENVELOPE** | ATTACK · DECAY · SUSTAIN · RELEASE. Hold Shift for PLAY · GLIDE · DRIFT · VELOCITY (see below) |
| **T3 FX** | The sound's own effect. Press T3 again to switch between ECHO, ROOM, CRUSH and SWIRL. Shift + T3 switches to the bank B effects (see below). |
| **T4 LFO** | **LFO 1**: RATE · AMOUNT · DEST · SHAPE. Hold Shift for **LFO 2** (see below) |

Each slot remembers its own engine settings, envelope, effect, LFOs and macro.

### LFO 1 and LFO 2: T4

Each sound has two LFOs. The T4 knobs set **LFO 1**; hold **Shift** for **LFO 2**. The screen shows both, LFO 1 on top and LFO 2 underneath, with the one you are editing lit.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: RATE** | 0.05 to 19 Hz |
| **Green: AMOUNT** | How much it moves its target. At 0 the LFO is off |
| **White: DEST** | **PITCH** (vibrato) · **AMP** (tremolo) · **PAN** · **TONE** (a filter sweep) · **FX MIX** (the sound's effect breathes in and out) · or any of the **engine's four knobs**, shown by name (CUTOFF, DETUNE, VOWEL…) |
| **Orange: SHAPE** | SINE · TRI · SQR · SAW · **RANDOM** (a new random level on each step, like sample and hold) · **DRIFT** (a smooth random wander) |

PITCH, AMP, PAN, TONE and FX MIX move all the time. An engine knob is read as each note starts, so the LFO changes the notes one by one. That is great on sequences and arpeggios, where every note comes out a little different. Both LFOs can run at once, for example a slow DRIFT on TONE and a fast SINE on PITCH.

### The macro: Shift + knobs on T1

Hold **Shift** on the T1 page to turn the sound's **MACRO**: one knob that moves several things at once. Each engine has its own macro, chosen so that turning it up makes the sound bigger, brighter or wilder. On NEON it adds detune and opens the cutoff; on ACID it opens the filter and adds resonance and envelope.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: MACRO** | The macro itself, 0–100 |
| **Green: ENGINE** | How far, and which way, the macro moves the engine's knobs (−100 to +100) |
| **White: TONE** | A low-pass that the macro opens (+) or closes (−) |
| **Orange: FX** | How much effect the macro adds (+) or takes away (−) |

The screen shows the macro dial and, for each engine knob, where it is (grey tick) and where the macro takes it (white line). Engine knobs change on the next notes you play; TONE and FX change at once. The macro is saved with the sound and in your presets. Sample slots keep Shift + T1 for the sample's own settings.

### Effects

Every synth slot and drum kit has one effect, chosen on the T3 page. There are 8 effects in two banks:

| Effect | Sound | Knobs |
|---|---|---|
| **ECHO** | Simple delay | TIME · REPEAT · TONE · MIX |
| **ROOM** | Room reverb | SIZE · DAMP · PRE · MIX |
| **CRUSH** | Bit crusher and drive | CRUSH · DRIVE · TONE · MIX |
| **SWIRL** | Phaser | RATE · DEPTH · FEEDBK · MIX |
| **BOUNCE** (⇧T3) | Ping-pong delay that bounces left and right, with tape-style wobble | TIME · WOBBLE · FEEDBK · MIX |
| **COIL** (⇧T3) | Spring reverb | TONE · TENSION · DAMP · MIX |
| **CUBE** (⇧T3) | Box-shaped room reverb; SHAPE goes from natural to metallic | SIZE · SHAPE · FEEDBK · MIX |
| **RADIO** (⇧T3) | Old radio or phone: narrow band, lower sample rate, crackle and dropouts | BAND · CRUSH · STATIC · MIX |

- **T3** opens the effect page. Press T3 again to step through ECHO, ROOM, CRUSH and SWIRL.
- **Shift + T3** (Shift + M) switches to bank B; the T3 light turns orange. Press Shift + T3 again to step through BOUNCE, COIL, CUBE and RADIO.
- Opening the effect page with T3 from another page keeps the sound's current effect, whichever bank it is in.
- Every effect screen shows its picture on the left and its two main values as big numbers on the right, in the colours of their knobs.
- Switching banks moves to the effect in the same position: ECHO ↔ BOUNCE, ROOM ↔ COIL, CRUSH ↔ CUBE, SWIRL ↔ RADIO.

### Synths & presets menu: Shift + SYNTH

Hold **Shift** and press **SYNTH** (Shift + Z) to open a menu of every synth with its presets. The SYNTH key lights orange while it is open.

- **Blue knob:** scroll through the 16 synth slots, bank A and bank B. The synth loads as soon as it is highlighted.
- **Green knob:** scroll through that synth's presets. Each one loads as you turn, so play the keys while you browse. **INIT** brings back the synth's default sound.
- Each synth has INIT plus 4 presets, for example E.PIANO, BELL, SLAP BASS and METAL on FM, or FANFARE, MELLOW, SFORZANDO and SECTION on BRASS.
- A preset sets the synth's four knobs, its envelope and its effect. You can tweak any of them afterwards; your changes are saved as usual.
- **White knob: ENGINE.** Picks the engine that plays in the slot you are on, from the whole engine library (see below). The slot starts from that engine's own sound, and the list on the green knob shows that engine's presets. The left of the screen shows ENGINE 17/20, for example.
- **Orange knob: RANDOM.** Each step you turn it makes a new random sound for the slot, kept within musical limits (a gentle envelope, a light effect, now and then a little LFO). The title shows RANDOM #3, for example. Save one you like with Shift + T1.
- Your own presets appear at the end of each synth's list, marked with a dot. A preset you save belongs to the engine it was made with, so it is there in any slot that plays that engine.
- Press **Shift + SYNTH** again, or T3 or T4, to close the menu.

#### Sounds by type: T2 in the menu

**T1** is the list by slot above; **T2** lists every sound by what it is, across all 24 engines, so you can look for "a bass" rather than a slot or an engine.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: TYPE** | **BASS · KEYS · PAD · LEAD · PLUCK · PERC · FX**, or **FAVOURITES** |
| **Green: SOUND** | Scrolls through every sound of that type, with its engine on the right. Each one loads as you turn, and plays a short preview that fits the type (a bass line, a chord, a riff), so you can hear it without playing |
| **White: FAVOURITE** | Stars the sound (turn right, or push); turn left to take the star off. Starred sounds have a ★ and are listed under FAVOURITES. Favourites are kept for every project, like your saved presets |
| **Orange: SLOT** | The slot the sound loads into |

Your own presets are listed under their engine's type.

### Saving your own presets: Shift + T1

In synth mode, hold **Shift** and press **T1** (Shift + B) to open **SAVE PRESET** for the synth you are on. It works from the synths & presets menu too. T1 lights orange while it is open. The preset is saved on that synth, in its bank, with its four knobs, envelope, effect and LFO.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: LETTER** | Picks the letter at the cursor: A–Z, 0–9, - or a space |
| **Green: CURSOR** | Moves along the name (10 characters) |
| **White: SAVE** | Push it, or turn it all the way right (from NO to YES), to save. A preset with the same name is overwritten |
| **Orange: DELETE** | When the name matches one of your presets, push it twice (the first push shows PUSH AGAIN), or turn it all the way right, to delete that preset |

On a computer you can also type the name on the keyboard (Backspace and ← → work too). **Enter** saves and closes, **Esc** closes without saving. While the screen is open the letter keys type instead of playing. If the current sound is one of your presets, its name is already filled in, so you can overwrite or delete it straight away. Each synth holds up to 16 of your presets.

### Key & chords: Shift + TEMPO

**No wrong notes, in one press:** in synth mode, **Shift + LOOP** steps through **SCALE LOCK** (every key snaps to the project's key and scale) → **CHORDS** (each key also plays the chord that fits it in the key) → **FREE PLAY** (both off). It remembers the scale and chord type you used last, and the title bar shows what is on (A MIN · TRIAD, for example). KEY & CHORDS below sets everything in detail.

Hold **Shift** and press **TEMPO** (Shift + `) to open **KEY & CHORDS**. The TEMPO key lights orange. Pick a key and a scale and every note you play stays in it; turn on a chord type and every key plays a whole chord that fits. It works on all the synths, from the computer keyboard, the on-screen keys and MIDI, and in the sequencers. Drum mode is not affected.

| Knob | Job | Shift + knob | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue: KEY** | The key, C to B | **VOICING** | CLOSE · INVERSION 1 · INVERSION 2 · OPEN (the middle note goes up an octave) · DROP (the root goes down an octave) |
| **Green: SCALE** | OFF · MAJOR · MINOR · DORIAN · MIXOLYDIAN · LYDIAN · PHRYGIAN · HARMONIC MINOR · MAJOR PENTA · MINOR PENTA · BLUES · WHOLE TONE | **STRUM** | Plays the chord's notes one after another, up to 120 ms apart, like a strummed guitar |
| **White: KEYS** | **SNAP**: the keyboard stays a normal piano and a note outside the scale moves to the nearest scale note. **WHITE KEYS**: the white keys walk up the scale from the root, so you can play any scale on the white keys alone (the black keys repeat the white key on their left) | **ADD BASS** | Adds the chord's root an octave below |
| **Orange: CHORD** | OFF · TRIAD · SEVENTH · NINTH (stacked from the scale, so each key gets the right chord for the key: in A minor, A plays Am, C plays C, B plays Bdim) · SUS2 · SUS4 · POWER · OCTAVES (fixed shapes on any note) | **KEY GUIDE** | Dots on the on-screen keys: grey on the scale notes, orange on the root |

With SCALE set to OFF, the chord types still follow the key's major scale, and the keys play every note.

**The screen:** the key is shown large in blue, with the scale, the key mode and the chord type under it. On the right are the chord you played last, in big orange letters, and its notes, and a two-octave keyboard where the notes that sound light up in blue and the scale notes have a dot (orange on the root). Along the bottom is the chord on every step of the scale (I to VII), so you can see the chords that belong to the key; the one you just played is boxed. While a scale or chord type is on, the title bar of every screen shows it, for example **A MIN · 7TH**.

**With the sequencers:** with ARP, holding one key arpeggiates its chord. In CHAIN, Shift + a key adds the whole chord as one step. ENDLESS and CHAIN take the scale note when you transpose them, and PATTERN toggles the whole chord on a step.

**Pages:** T1–T3 pick the page of KEY & CHORDS: T1 KEY (above), T2 PROGRESSION, T3 FOLLOW BASS. T4 closes it.

#### T2: PROGRESSION

Makes a chord progression in your key and puts it into the **CHAIN** sequencer, ready to play.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: STYLE** | POP (I–V–vi–IV) · DREAMY (I–iii–vi–IV) · SAD (vi–IV–I–V) · EPIC (i–VI–III–VII) · FIFTIES (I–vi–IV–V) · JAZZY (ii–V–I–vi) · CIRCLE (eight chords around the circle of fifths) · DICE (a new progression each time, from chords that lead well into each other) |
| **Green: CHORDS** | 4 or 8 |
| **White: MAKE** | Push it, or turn it all the way to YES, to write the progression |
| **Orange: EACH** | How long each chord lasts: 2 beats, 1 bar or 2 bars |

The chords are built from the scale of your key, with the CHORD type (TRIAD, SEVENTH or NINTH; triads otherwise) and the VOICING. The style names describe the steps in a major key; in a minor key the same steps give the minor version, so POP in A minor is Am – Em – F – Dm. The screen shows the chords as boxes with their names and steps, and lights the one that plays. Tap the **low C** to play the progression: HOLD is turned on, so it keeps going until you press STOP. Another key transposes it. Making a progression replaces what was in CHAIN.

#### T3: FOLLOW BASS

Another synth slot plays a bass line under whatever you play: your chords on the keys, or the CHAIN, PATTERN and ARP sequencers. It follows the lowest note that sounds, in time with the tempo.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: SLOT** | OFF, or the synth slot that plays the bass (A1–B8), with its own sound and envelope |
| **Green: STYLE** | **ROOT** (one long note per chord) · **PULSE** (every 8th) · **OFFBEAT** (between the beats) · **OCTAVES** (low and high) · **WALK** (root, fifth, octave, fifth on the beats) |
| **White: OCTAVE** | −2, −1 or 0 octaves from the keyboard's low C |
| **Orange: LEVEL** | How loud the bass plays |

The title bar shows **BASS B1**, for example, while it is on. The bass plays through the current sound's effect, and it records onto the tape together with what you play. Make a progression on T2, pick a bass slot on T3, tap the low C, and you have chords and a bass line from one key.

### The engine library: 24 engines for 16 slots

Any engine can play in any slot. The 16 slots start with the engines in the tables above, and eight more engines are in the library. To put one in a slot, open the synths & presets menu (Shift + SYNTH) and turn the **white knob**.

| Engine | Sound | Knobs | Presets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PLUCK** | Plucked string with a wooden body: guitar, harp, koto, muted plucks. The high notes ring shorter, like a real string | BRIGHT · DECAY · BODY · PICK (where the string is plucked) | NYLON · HARP · KOTO · MUTED |
| **TINE** | Electric piano: a warm body that barks when you dig in, a metallic tine on the attack, a natural decay and a tremolo that swings left and right | BARK · TINE · TREMOLO · TONE | CLASSIC · BARK · DREAMY · DUSTY |
| **BOOM** | Deep sub bass for hip-hop and trap: it drops into each note from above, glides from the last note while one is still held, and is driven so it is heard on small speakers. It plays one note at a time | PUNCH · DRIVE · GLIDE · TONE | CLASSIC · DISTORTED · LONG GLIDE · DEEP SUB |
| **MALLET** | Struck bars: marimba, vibraphone, glass and kalimba. MATERIAL moves from wood through metal to glass, HARDNESS is the mallet, TREMOLO is the vibraphone's motor | MATERIAL · HARDNESS · DECAY · TREMOLO | MARIMBA · VIBES · GLASS · KALIMBA |
| **GRAIN** | Granular: many tiny pieces of a sound played over each other, from clouds to frozen drones to stutters. It uses the sample loaded in the slot (load one while GRAIN is in the slot), or a built-in texture | POSITION · SIZE · DENSITY · SPRAY | CLOUD · FREEZE · SHIMMER · STUTTER |
| **VOWEL** | A small choir through formant filters that move from A to E, I, O and U: choir pads, talking leads, whispers | VOWEL · SIZE · BREATH · VIBRATO | CHOIR AAH · OOH PAD · ROBOT · WHISPER |

| **FELT** | Soft piano, synthesized (no samples): stretched partials that fade at their own rates, a felt hammer, two strings beating, and an optional tape wobble for lo-fi keys | TONE · DECAY · FELT · WOBBLE | SOFT · UPRIGHT · LO-FI KEYS · MUSIC BOX |

Changing a slot's engine replaces that slot's sound, so save it as a preset first (Shift + T1) if you want to keep it. Each project remembers which engine is in each slot.

### Play modes, glide, drift and velocity: Shift + T2

On the ENVELOPE page (T2) of a synth, hold **Shift** and turn the knobs. The settings belong to the slot, so each sound keeps its own.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: PLAY** | **POLY** plays chords (the usual). **MONO** plays one note at a time, the last key you press. **LEGATO** keeps one voice sounding and glides from note to note while you hold keys, and back when you let go of the top one, like a classic mono lead. **UNISON** plays three voices per note, detuned and spread left and right, for a huge sound |
| **Green: GLIDE** | How long the pitch takes to slide between notes in MONO and LEGATO, from OFF to 800 ms |
| **White: DRIFT** | A slow random pitch wander for each voice, so chords breathe like an old analog synth |
| **Orange: VELOCITY** | How much your touch (MIDI or the sequencers' velocity) changes the level. At 0 every note is equally loud |

The envelope screen shows the settings while Shift is held, and the title bar shows the play mode when it isn't POLY. CHIP's arpeggio keeps its own pitch steps, so with CHIP, LEGATO plays like MONO.

Bank B is reached from synth mode only: press **SYNTH** (Z) first, then Shift + 1–8. In drum mode Shift + 4 opens the pad sample editor instead, and in the other modes Shift + 1–8 does nothing.

## 4. Drum mode

Press **DRUM** (X), then pick a kit with **1–8**: BOX · DEEP · TINY · DIRT · TAPE · CLICK · AIR · ROOM.

Every musical key plays a different drum sound, and the screen flashes the pad you hit.

| Page | Knobs |
|---|---|
| **T1** | TUNE · DECAY · TONE · DRIVE. Hold Shift for **MODEL** and **CHOKE** (see below). Press T1 again for the **SAMPLER** of the selected pad |
| **T2 MIX** | KICK · SNARE · HATS · PERC levels |
| **T3 FX** | The kit's effect (press T3 again to change it, or Shift + T3 for bank B) |
| **T4 LFO** | Same as for synths: LFO 1, and LFO 2 with Shift |

### Kit models and choke

On the T1 page, hold **Shift** and turn the knobs:

- **Blue: MODEL** changes how the kit's twelve sounds are made, so each of the 8 kits can sound four ways. The kit's TUNE, DECAY, TONE, DRIVE and MIX still apply.
  - **CLASSIC:** the kit's own sounds.
  - **ANALOG:** a classic analog drum machine: a long boomy kick, a two-tone snare, a clap made of quick bursts, hats and cymbals from six metallic oscillators, and a cowbell.
  - **PUNCH:** tight and bright: a short kick with a click, a noisy snare, brighter hats and a ride on the BELL pad.
  - **DUSTY:** the analog sounds through a bit crusher and a dull filter, with a little hiss, for lo-fi beats.
- **Green: CHOKE** (on by default): the closed hat cuts off a ringing open hat, like on a real kit.

The title shows the model when it isn't CLASSIC, for example **DRUM 1 · BOX · ANALOG**.

### Pad samples

Each kit can have your own sample on any pad, so you could put kick.wav on KICK and keep the built-in sounds everywhere else.

1. In drum mode, pick a kit and hit the pad you want to replace. The screen rings the selected pad.
2. Click **load sample** (or drag an audio file onto the page), or **record sample** to record one from the microphone.
3. The sample now plays on that pad only, in that kit. A small square on the pad and the file name at the top of the screen show it.

The kit's TUNE changes its pitch and TONE how bright it is. How long it plays is set in the pad sample editor (Shift + 4), with START, END and the play mode. On the upper row of keys it plays higher, like the built-in sounds. **clear sample** puts the built-in sound back on the selected pad. Pad samples are saved, and **reset all** erases them.

#### Editing a pad sample: Shift + 4

Key 4 is labelled **SAMPLE** in orange. In drum mode, hold **Shift** and press **4** (or press T1 again on the T1 page) to open the **SAMPLER** for the selected pad. Its T1 EDIT page is the sample editor below; T2–T4 slice, record and take samples from the tape (see section 5). On EDIT it works as you would edit a sample in synth mode. Key 4 lights orange while it is open, and the screen shows the sample's waveform with the part that plays in white. While the pad plays, an orange line runs through the waveform, one for each hit that is still sounding. The play mode is shown in the top corner, fades are drawn as orange ramps, and a green line under the waveform marks a loop.

| Knob | What it does | Shift + knob | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue: START** | Where the sample starts playing | **PLAY** | **ONE SHOT** plays the whole part from one hit · **GATE** plays only while the key is held · **LOOP** loops the part while the key is held |
| **Green: END** | Where it stops | **FADE IN** | Fades the start in, up to 2 s |
| **White: TUNE** | Pitch, ±12 semitones | **FADE OUT** | Fades the end out, up to 2 s (ONE SHOT and GATE) |
| **Orange: REVERSE** | Plays the sample backwards | **LEVEL** | The pad's volume |

The kit's TUNE, TONE and level still shape a sampled pad. Its length now comes from START, END and the play mode, so the kit's DECAY knob no longer cuts samples short. When a sequencer plays a GATE or LOOP pad, the pad stops at the end of each step.

Hit another pad to edit that pad's sample; pads without a sample show a reminder to load one. Loading a sample while the editor is open puts it on the selected pad. Press **Shift + 4** again, or T1 on the EDIT page, to close the sampler. The settings are saved with the pad, and loading a new sample on a pad resets them.

### Drum machine

Press **Shift + DRUM** (Shift + X) to open the drum machine. The DRUM light turns orange. It has 12 pads (KICK to SHAKE), each built from **two synth voices and a filter**, plus a **16-step pattern**. Keys play the pads and select the one you edit. The left of the screen always shows the pad grid, the pad's name and a live waveform.

| Page | Knobs | Screen |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 VOICE 1** | TUNE · WAVE (sine, triangle, square, noise) · DECAY · BEND (pitch drop) | Both voices side by side, with a tune dial, wave shape and decay and pitch curves |
| **T2 VOICE 2** | Same, for the second voice | Same, with voice 2 highlighted |
| **T3 FILTER** | MODE (off, low, high, band, notch, peak, ring, crush) · CUTOFF · RESO · MIX (voice 1 ↔ voice 2) | Mode dial and the filter's response curve |
| **T4 STEPS** | PAD · LENGTH (1–16 steps, for this pattern) · PAD LEN (the selected pad's own length) · RUN | A 16-step grid for all 12 pads, with the playhead, and the 8 patterns on the left |

- On **STEPS**, the first 16 keys switch steps on and off for the selected pad. Hold Shift and press a key to pick a different pad.
- **Edit a step:** hold its key and turn the knobs. The title shows the step, and the knobs become **VELOCITY** (how loud), **CHANCE** (how often it plays, from never to always), **ROLL** (1 to 4 quick hits in the step, for hat rolls) and **NUDGE** (a little early or late). Letting go of a step you turned on and didn't change keeps it; tapping a step that was on turns it off.
- On the grid, a quiet step is dimmer, a step with less than 100% chance is drawn hollow and filled as far as its chance, a roll shows as thin lines inside the step, and a nudged step sits a little left or right.
- **Patterns:** hold **Shift** on STEPS and the knobs become **PATTERN** (1–8, the one you edit), **CHAIN**, **HITS** and **ROTATE**. Each pattern has its own steps and length. The boxes on the left show the 8 patterns: the one playing is orange, the one you edit is outlined, and the ones with steps are grey. Choosing another pattern while it runs switches at the end of the current one.
- **CHAIN:** OFF, or 2 to 8 to play patterns 1 to that number in turn (a green line under them), for an intro, a verse and a fill.
- **HITS** spreads that many hits as evenly as possible over the pattern for the selected pad, a quick way to build rhythms (3 hits over 8 steps gives a classic syncopated rhythm). **ROTATE** moves the pad's steps one step later or earlier.
- The closed hat (HAT) cuts off a ringing open hat (OPEN).
- The pattern follows the tempo and swing. It keeps playing after you close the drum machine, and it records onto the tape like anything else.
- **Patterns on the keys:** with the drum machine open, **1–8** pick the pattern to edit, and **Shift + 1–8** copies the pattern you are on to that number (handy for a variation or a fill).
- **Step locks:** hold a step, then press **T1**, **T2** or **T3**. The knobs become that page's knobs (VOICE 1, VOICE 2 or FILTER) for this step only. Turn one to lock its value on the step, for example a higher TUNE on one snare hit or another filter on one hat. Locked values show a dot, and the voice and filter pictures show the step's sound. Push a knob (double-click) to clear its lock. A step with locks has an orange dot on the grid. Press **T4** while holding to go back to VELOCITY · CHANCE · ROLL · NUDGE.
- **Conditions:** while holding a step, hold **Shift** too and turn the blue knob. **COND** makes the step play only on some passes of the pattern: **1 OF 2** (the first of every two passes), **2 OF 2**, **1 OF 3** … **4 OF 4**, **FILL** (only during a fill), **NO FILL** (never during a fill) or **FIRST** (only the first pass after you start). Steps with a condition have a small mark in the corner, orange for FILL ones.
- **Pad lengths:** **PAD LEN** (white knob on STEPS) gives the selected pad its own length, from 1 to 16 steps; push the knob to set it back to **ALL** (the pattern's length). A pad with its own length loops over just those steps while the others carry on, so a 12-step hat over a 16-step kick drifts against it and the groove keeps shifting, a bar at a time. The drift carries on from pass to pass and starts again when the pattern changes. On the grid, steps past a pad's length are dimmed, a thin line marks where it ends, and each pad shows its own playhead. **HITS** and **ROTATE** work within the pad's own length.
- **DICE & FILL:** press **T4** again on STEPS for the **DICE & FILL** page (T4 lights orange):
  - **Blue: DICE** rolls a new pattern: kick on the one, snare on the backbeat, hats on the 8ths (16ths when it is busy), an open hat now and then, and tom fills marked FILL at the end. Turn it back to return to earlier rolls, and forward again to replay them; past the last one it rolls a new one. Push it to roll at once.
  - **Green: WHAT:** ALL PADS, or THIS PAD to roll only the selected pad.
  - **White: DENSITY:** how busy the rolls are.
  - **Orange: FILL:** OFF, or EVERY 2, 4 or 8 passes: the last pass of each group is a fill.
  - Hold **Shift** on this page for the drum machine's **LEVEL** (blue knob).
- **Fills by hand:** hold **→** while the drum machine is open. FILL steps play, NO FILL steps rest, and FILL lights up in orange on the screen. Fills also work for the PATTERN sequencer's FILL steps.
- Press **Shift + DRUM** again to close it. Your pads and all 8 patterns, with their locks and conditions, are saved with the project.

## 5. Sampler

Any synth slot can hold a sample instead of its engine.

- **load sample:** pick an audio file. You can also drag an audio file onto the page.
- **browse samples:** pick a folder; its audio files (WAV, MP3, AIFF, FLAC, OGG, M4A) are listed on the screen. Turn the blue knob to scroll, and each one plays a short preview (up to 4 s) as you land on it; **HEAR** (green) plays it again; **LOAD** (white) puts it in the synth slot, or on the selected pad in drum mode. On the WEB-1 device, the USB stick's sample list works the same way (SYSTEM → STORAGE → LOAD SAMPLE FROM USB).
- **record sample:** record from the microphone, or from the line input when INPUT is set to LINE IN in advanced REC (Shift + REC, then Shift + orange knob).
- **clear sample:** removes the sample and brings the slot's engine back.

The sample plays across the keys at different pitches. While it plays, an orange line runs through the waveform, one for each note that sounds.

| Knob | Job | Shift + knob | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue: START** | Where it starts | **PLAY** | **ONE SHOT** plays the whole part from one tap · **GATE** plays only while the key is held · **LOOP** loops the part while the key is held |
| **Green: END** | Where it ends | **FADE IN** | Fades the start in, up to 2 s |
| **White: TUNE** | ±12 semitones | **FADE OUT** | Fades the end out, up to 2 s (ONE SHOT and GATE) |
| **Orange: REVERSE** | Plays backwards | **LEVEL** | Volume |

The screen and knobs work exactly like the drum pad editor (Shift + 4 in drum mode): the slot and the sample's length at the top left, the play mode and a light that blinks on each note at the top right, fades as orange ramps, a green line under the waveform for a loop, and the waveform flipped when REVERSE is on, so the part that plays always reads from left to right.

### The SAMPLER pages: T1 again

Press **T1** again on the T1 page to open the **SAMPLER**: in synth mode it works on the slot, in drum mode on the selected pad (Shift + 4 in drum mode opens it too). T1–T4 pick its page; T1 on the EDIT page closes it.

| Page | Knobs | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 EDIT** | START · END · TUNE · REVERSE; Shift: PLAY · FADE IN · FADE OUT · LEVEL | The sample editor described above |
| **T2 SLICE** | MODE · SLICES · SLICE · SENSE; Shift: BARS · FIT · KEEP PITCH | Cuts the sample (between START and END) into slices and puts them on the pads of the current kit, slice 1 on the lowest key. **EVEN** cuts 2 to 24 equal parts; **AT HITS** cuts where the sound jumps up, such as each hit of a drum loop, up to the SLICES number. **SENSE** sets how small a jump counts. The screen shows the cuts on the waveform. Push SLICE, or turn it to YES, to do it |
| **T3 RECORD** | SOURCE · LENGTH · REC · START | Resampling: records **MASTER** (everything you hear, effects and all) or the **INPUT** (the mic or line in, as set in advanced REC) into the slot or the pad. LENGTH: 1 BEAT, 2 BEATS, 1 BAR, 2 BARS or 4 BARS at the tempo (up to 10 s in a slot, 6 s on a pad). START: NOW, or on the NEXT BAR so the sample starts on the beat. Push REC to start; push it again to stop early |
| **T4 FROM TAPE** | FROM · TRACK · TAKE | Takes a piece of the tape into the sampler: the **LIFTED** piece (the last LIFT or COPY), the **LOOP** area or the **BAR** at the tape head, from one **TRACK** or ALL four mixed at their levels. Push TAKE |

**Fit to tempo:** hold **Shift** on the SLICE page. The part of the sample between START and END is fitted to a whole number of bars at the tempo, so a loop from anywhere plays in time with your song.

- **Blue: BARS:** **AUTO** picks the number of bars closest to the loop's length (1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, 4 or 8), or pick one yourself.
- **Green: FIT:** push it, or turn it to YES, to do it. The sample then lasts exactly that many bars, and START and END go back to the whole sample.
- **White: KEEP PITCH:** **ON** stretches the time and keeps the pitch; the loop is stretched a 16th note at a time, so every hit on the grid lands on the new grid. **OFF** changes the speed like tape, so the pitch moves too.
- The screen shows the loop's length, what it will become, and the new bars and beats over the waveform.

**One-press resample:** in drum mode, **Shift + 5** (labelled **RESAMPLE**) records what you hear onto the selected pad, for the LENGTH set on the RECORD page. While the tape, the drum machine or a sequencer plays, it starts on the next bar; otherwise straight away. Press it again to stop early.

**Straight from the tape:** in tape mode, **Shift + 4** sends the piece you just lifted (LIFT) or copied (Shift + T1) to the sampler: to the synth slot you are on, or to the selected pad when DRUM is the instrument.

A recorded or taken sample is set to full level and starts exactly where it was cut, so loops stay in time. Slices keep their levels relative to each other.

## 6. Tape mode

Press **TAPE** (C). The tape has **4 tracks**, **1 minute** long in a new project. Each project can have a longer tape: **PROJECTS → TAPE LENGTH** (Shift + 7 in tape mode), turn the orange knob to **1, 2, 4 or 6 MIN** and push DO IT. WEB-1 saves the tape and opens the project again at the new length, with everything on it kept. Making it shorter cuts what is after the new end, so that takes two pushes. A long tape uses more memory (about 46 MB a minute), so on a phone shorter is safer.

- **T1–T4** pick the track to record on. The selected track's number is shown large in the top-left corner, in its colour.
- **REC** arms the tape and starts it. Whatever you play is recorded onto the selected track, on top of what is already there.
- **PLAY** plays the tape. **Shift + PLAY** plays it backwards.
- **STOP** stops it; press STOP twice to go back to the start.
- **← / →:** hold to wind the tape.

**Knobs:** JOG (move the tape head) · LOOP (loop length in bars) · REC LVL · SPEED.

SPEED works like real tape: record at one speed and play back at another, and the pitch changes.

**The tape screen** comes in two views; pick one in **SYSTEM → DISPLAY → TAPE VIEW**:

- **TAPE FOCUS** (the default): two big reels that turn as the tape moves, with the tape pack growing on the right as it plays, and the tape running over two rollers and the head. Between the reels: the time, the bar and beat, and the section you are in (from SECTIONS). On the left, the selected track's number in its colour, the **FX** box (orange while any master effect is on) and any recording settings that are on; on the right, the tape SPEED. Under the tape, each track is one thin line in its colour where it has audio, with bar ticks, the loop, the section markers and the red head.
- **TRACK FOCUS**: small reels and the time on top, the section you are in and what comes next ("NEXT OUTRO IN 6 BARS"), the play state, FX and speed. Under them, a strip of the whole 60-second tape with the sections in their colours, the loop and the head, then four tall lanes: each shows its number, MUTE, SOLO or REC, its mixer level, and its audio around the head with bar lines.

In both, holding Shift shows the four edits (T1–T4) and outlines the bar they will use, and a tape trick shows its name while you hold it.

The tape screen has no row of knob values along the bottom, so both views use the whole height. The four knobs still work as usual (JOG, LOOP, REC LVL, SPEED); the time, the loop and the SPEED gauge show their effect.

### Advanced REC: Shift + REC

Hold **Shift** and press **REC** (Shift + R) to open **ADVANCED REC**. The REC light turns orange. The screen shows the REC disc and the selected track on the left (READY, RECORDING, or blinking while it waits), and one panel per knob.

| Knob | Job | Shift + knob | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue: SOURCE** | What is recorded: INSTRUMENT (what you play), MIC (or LINE IN, see INPUT), or BOTH. The panel shows the input level | **THRESHOLD** | How loud the input must get to start an ON SOUND recording (the white line on the level meter) |
| **Green: KEEP OLD** | What happens to what is already on the track where you record: 100% overdubs (the default), 0% replaces it, in between fades it a little more with each pass | **MONITOR** | How loud you hear the microphone or line in while you record |
| **White: START** | KEY starts as soon as you press REC · ON SOUND rolls the tape and starts recording when the input passes the threshold · NEXT BAR rolls the tape and starts on the next bar line | **PUNCH** | LOOP AREA records only between the loop markers, even with LOOP off |
| **Orange: AUTO STOP** | OFF, or stop recording after 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars; the tape keeps playing | **INPUT** | **MIC** or **LINE IN**: which input SOURCE records from. On the WEB-1 hardware, LINE IN is the IN jack; on a computer it picks your audio interface, or else the second input. The sampler's **record sample** uses it too, and the REC panel shows a jack instead of a microphone |

Press **REC** again while it waits to cancel, or **STOP**. The settings are saved, and any that are not the usual ones are listed in small text at the top right of the tape screen, for example REPLACE, MIC or STOP 2 BAR. The microphone and line in need your permission and work on `localhost` or HTTPS. The count-in is on the tempo screen (green knob).

### Erasing the whole tape: Shift + TAPE

Hold **Shift** and press **TAPE** (Shift + C) to open **ERASE TAPE?**. The TAPE key lights orange. To go ahead, **hold T1, T2, T3 and T4 down together** (on a computer, hold **B N M ,**). Each box lights in its track colour while its key is held and goes out again when you let go, so tapping the keys one by one does nothing. As soon as all four are held at once, the tape is wiped, with a progress bar. There is no undo. Until all four are held, **Shift + TAPE** again or any mode key cancels. Musical keys don't play while the prompt is open.

### System menu: Shift + 8

In tape mode, hold **Shift** and press **8** (labelled **MENU**) to open the system menu. Key 8 lights orange. It is laid out like the synths & presets menu: a picture of the section on the left, the sections in blue, the items and their values in green.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: SECTION** | BATTERY · TIME · TIMEZONE · STORAGE · DISPLAY · MIDI · CPU · ABOUT |
| **Green: ITEM** | The setting inside the section |
| **White: SET / CONFIRM / OPEN** | Changes the setting. To confirm an action, turn it all the way to YES or push it; the ERASE items, POWER OFF and INSTALL UPDATE take two pushes |

| Section | Items |
|---|---|
| **BATTERY** | Level, charging or on battery, and time left, where the browser reports a battery. On the hardware: the real level and charging state from the charger, the cell voltage, and **POWER OFF**, which saves everything and then turns WEB-1 off |
| **TIME** | Hour, minute, 12 or 24 hour clock, date, and USE DEVICE TIME to go back to the real clock. On the hardware, these set the device's own clock |

With VOICE LIMIT on AUTO, WEB-1 plays fewer notes at once when the device struggles, and gives them back when it recovers; the title bar shows CPU while it holds back. Or pick a fixed limit from 4 to 16 voices |

The settings are saved. In the browser, battery and storage show what the browser can see. The items marked "on the hardware" appear when WEB-1 runs on the device (see [On the WEB-1 hardware](#16-on-the-web-1-hardware)).

**Looping:** **Q** sets a loop starting at the tape head, with the length set by the green knob.

**Editing:**

- **LIFT** (`[`) cuts the loop, or the current bar, out of the selected track.
- **DROP** (`]`) pastes it at the tape head.
- **Shift + LIFT** erases the whole track.
- **Shift + DROP** (Shift + ]) is **UNDO**: it puts back the track as it was before the last tape change (and undoes any other change too, see [Undo](#undo-shift--drop)). **Shift + STOP** is **REDO**.

**More edits: Shift + T1–T4** work on the selected track, over the loop if LOOP is on, otherwise over the bar at the tape head. While you hold Shift, the tape screen shows the four edits and outlines the bar they will use.

Thin vertical lines on the tracks mark every bar (every fourth bar is brighter), with fainter lines for the beats, so you can see where to cut and loop.

| Keys | Edit | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Shift + T1** | COPY | Copies the part without cutting it. Press **DROP** to paste it at the tape head. |
| **Shift + T2** | CROP | Keeps only that part of the track and erases everything else. |
| **Shift + T3** | DOUBLE | Pastes the part again right after itself, to repeat a bar or a loop. |
| **Shift + T4** | ERASE | Clears only that part of the track. |

**Tape tricks:** in tape mode, hold one of these keys while the tape plays:

- **1:** BRAKE, slows the tape to a stop.
- **2:** STUTTER, repeats a tiny slice.
- **3:** REVERSE, plays backwards while held.

### Sections: Shift + LOOP

Mark the parts of your song (intro, verse, chorus…) and jump or loop between them while it plays. In tape mode, hold **Shift** and press **LOOP** (Shift + Q) to open **SECTIONS**.

- Press **1–6** to set a marker at the tape head, on the nearest bar line. Press a number that is already set to **jump** there: while the tape plays, the jump happens on the next bar line so it stays in time; when the tape is stopped, the head goes there at once.
- With **LOOP** on, pressing a marker's number loops that section, from its marker to the next one.
- The screen shows the whole tape as a song map: each section in its colour with its name, the recorded audio, the loop in green and the tape head in red. Under it, the six keys show which markers are set; the one waiting to jump blinks.

| Knob | Job |
|---|---|
| **Blue: MARKER** | The marker the other knobs change (1–6) |
| **Green: NAME** | INTRO · VERSE · CHORUS · BRIDGE · BREAK · OUTRO · BUILD · DROP · HOOK · SOLO · A · B · C · D |
| **White: MOVE** | Moves the marker by a bar |
| **Orange: CLEAR** | Push twice, or turn to YES, to remove the marker |

The markers also show as small flags with their names on the tape screen. They are saved with the project, and erasing the whole tape clears them. You can't jump while recording.

#### Song mode: T2 on SECTIONS

Build a whole song from parts, and let WEB-1 play it for you. On SECTIONS, **T1** is the markers above, **T2** is the **SONG** page and **T3** switches **SONG ON** or off.

- A song is a list of up to 16 **parts**, for example INTRO 4 bars → VERSE 8 → CHORUS 8 → BRIDGE 4 → CHORUS 8 → OUTRO 4. A new song starts with four parts to change.
- Each part says which **drum machine pattern** plays (or none), which **mixer scene** comes in (or KEEP the mix as it is), and whether the **chords** play: the progression in the CHAIN sequencer (make one in KEY & CHORDS → PROGRESSION), with FOLLOW BASS if you set it.
- With **SONG ON**, **PLAY** runs the parts in order along the tape, from the tape's beginning: each part starts exactly on its bar, and the song stops at the end of the last part. **REC** records it onto the selected track, so you can lay down the whole arrangement and play over it on another track.
- With SONG ON, the parts are also written as the tape's section markers (the first six), so the tape screen and the markers page show the song.

| Knob | Job | Shift + knob | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue: PART** | Picks a part; past the last one, **+ ADD** (push it to add a copy of the last part) | **NAME** | INTRO · VERSE · CHORUS · BRIDGE… (the section names) |
| **Green: BARS** | How long the part is, 1–32 bars | **CHORDS** | ON or OFF |
| **White: DRUMS** | OFF, or the drum machine's PATTERN 1–8 | **MOVE** | Moves the part earlier or later in the song |
| **Orange: SCENE** | KEEP, or mixer SCENE 1–4 (store scenes with Shift + 5–8 in mixer mode) | **DELETE** | Push twice to remove the part |

The screen shows the song as a row of blocks, as wide as their bars, with the part that plays lit and a red line that follows the tape. Above it: the number of parts, the bars and the song's length, in orange if the song is longer than the tape.

## 7. Mixer mode

Press **MIXER** (V). Each T page gives the four knobs a different job, and holding **Shift** while you turn a knob reaches a second set of settings on every page. The title bar shows what Shift does on the page.

| Page | Knobs | Shift + knobs |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 LEVEL & PAN** | Track 1–4 level | Track 1–4 pan, left or right |
| **T2 MULTIBAND COMP** | LOW · MID · HIGH compression amount · MAKEUP gain | LOW X and HIGH X crossover frequencies · ATTACK · RELEASE |
| **T3 EQ** | Gain of the 4 bands: LOW · LO MID · HI MID · HIGH (±12 dB) | Slide each band's frequency |
| **T4 MASTER** | OUT · COMP · INSTR (live-playing level) · LIMIT | CEILING · LIM REL (limiter release) · COMP SPD · WIDTH (0–200%) |

- **T1** shows four channel strips: a fader and level meter for each track, with its pan underneath.
- **T2** splits the sound into three bands. The screen shows each band's level, its threshold line and how much it is being turned down (in orange). With LOW, MID and HIGH at 0 the compressor is off and the sound passes untouched.
- **T3** draws the real EQ curve; each coloured dot is a band, and holding Shift shows rings around the dots while you slide them.
- **Mute and solo:** in mixer mode, keys **1–4 mute** tracks 1–4 (their lights turn orange) and keys **5–8 solo** them (their lights come on). A muted track, or one left out of a solo, is dimmed on the mixer and on the tape screen, and the title bar shows **MUTE** or **SOLO**. Mute and solo also apply when you export the tape.
- **T4**: LIMIT pushes the mix into a limiter that never goes above the CEILING, so the mix gets louder without clipping. At 0 it is off. WIDTH narrows the stereo image (0% is mono) or widens it (up to 200%). The screen shows the output meter with the ceiling, and how much the compressor and limiter are turning the sound down.

### Scenes: Shift + 1–8 in mixer mode

A scene remembers the whole mix and the master FX: levels, pans, mutes and solos, sends, sidechain, EQ, compressors, master settings and the FX rack. In mixer mode, hold **Shift** and press **5–8** to store the mix in scene 1–4, and **Shift + 1–4** to bring a scene back. Mutes, solos and the FX on/off switches change at once, the knobs glide to their new places over one beat so the change sounds smooth, and the FX settings land at the end. The title bar flashes SCENE 2 or SCENE 2 STORED. Scenes are saved with the project.

### Mixer bank B: Shift + MIXER

Hold **Shift** and press **MIXER** (Shift + V) for a second set of pages. The MIXER key lights orange. Shift + MIXER again goes back.

| Page | Knobs | Shift + knobs |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 SENDS** | REVERB 1–4: how much of each track goes to a reverb | DELAY 1–4: how much goes to a delay of 3/16 note that follows the tempo |
| **T2 SIDECHAIN** | SOURCE · DEPTH · RELEASE · THRESHOLD | |
| **T3 BOUNCE** | ONTO (the track to mix onto) · LEVEL · BOUNCE (push, or turn to YES) | |
| **T4 EXPORT** | EXPORT (TAPE MIX · STEMS · LIVE MIXDOWN) · GO (push, or turn to YES) | |

- **Sends:** the reverb and delay come back into the master, before the FX rack. Each track's two bars show its sends; Shift switches the knobs between reverb and delay.
- **Sidechain:** pick a **SOURCE**, either a tape track (for example the drums on track 1) or **LIVE KICK** (the kick of the kits and the drum machine as you play), and every other tape track dips each time the source hits, then comes back: the classic pumping sound. **DEPTH** is how far they dip, **RELEASE** how fast they come back, **THRESHOLD** how loud the source has to be. The screen draws the shape of one dip and shows each track dipping live; the source track is marked KEY. Live playing isn't ducked.
- **Bounce:** press keys **1–4** to choose the tracks to mix (they light up), pick the track to mix them **ONTO** and the **LEVEL**, then push **BOUNCE** (or turn it all the way to YES). The chosen tracks are mixed with their levels onto that track, added to what it already holds, so you can free tracks for more recording: clear the old ones afterwards with Shift + LIFT. **Shift + DROP** undoes a bounce.
- **Export:** **TAPE MIX** saves the whole tape as one stereo WAV with levels, pans, mutes, sends, a track sidechain and the master. **STEMS** saves each track that has audio as its own WAV, dry and all the same length, to finish the song in another app (the browser may ask once to allow several downloads). **LIVE MIXDOWN** records exactly what you hear, playing and knob moves included, until you turn GO again (up to 10 minutes); the title bar shows **●MIX** while it records.

## 8. Sequencers

Press **=** to open the sequencer screen, then choose a type with T1, T2, T3 or T4. **Shift + =** clears the sequence.

- **ENDLESS (T1):**
  - Hold Shift and tap notes to add steps.
  - Then hold any key to play the steps, transposed to that key.
  - Knobs: RATE · GATE · DIR (FWD, REV, PING, RND) · LATCH.
- **PATTERN (T2):**
  - A step grid. The blue knob picks a step; tap keys to switch notes on or off on that step.
  - The orange knob starts and stops the pattern.
  - Knobs: STEP · RATE · LENGTH · RUN.
  - **Step settings:** hold **Shift** and the knobs set the selected step's **VELOCITY** · **CHANCE** · **ROLL** (1–4 quick repeats) · **COND** (the same conditions as the drum machine: 1 OF 2 … 4 OF 4, FILL, NO FILL, FIRST). Quiet steps are drawn dimmer, a line under a step shows its chance, and a corner mark shows a condition.
  - **Accent and slide:** turn **VELOCITY** past 100% for **ACCENT**: the step plays louder and the sound's brightness knob (CUTOFF, TONE, BRIGHT…) opens up for that step. Turn **ROLL** below 1× for **SLIDE**: the note of the step before is held and glides into this step's note instead of playing again; slide several steps in a row for a long glide. Accented steps have an orange triangle under them, and a white line joins a slid step to the one before. Together they make the classic squelchy bassline on ACID or LADDER.
  - **Locks:** press **T2** again for **LOCKS**: the knobs become the sound's four engine knobs (CUTOFF, DETUNE…) for the selected step only. Turn one to lock it; push it (double-click) to clear it. Press T2 again to go back. Steps with locks have an orange dot.
  - **←** and **→** pick the step (on both pages), so they don't bend the pitch while PATTERN is on screen.
- **ARP (T3):**
  - Hold a chord and it is played as an arpeggio.
  - Knobs: RATE · GATE · MODE (UP, DOWN, UPDN, RND) · RANGE (1–3 octaves).
- **CHAIN (T4):** an endless step sequencer you write note by note, with chords, long notes and rests.
  - **Add notes:** hold **Shift** and press keys. Keys pressed together become one chord step. Keep Shift held until you have added all the notes you want.
  - **Long notes:** add a note with Shift, keep the key held and press **→**; each press makes that step one step longer.
  - **Rest:** **Shift + →** adds an empty step.
  - **Undo:** **Shift + ←** removes the last step.
  - **Play:** press any key and the chain plays transposed to it; the low C on the keyboard plays it as written. With HOLD on it keeps playing after you let go.
  - The screen shows the number of steps, the rate and gate, and the chain itself: chord notes stacked in each step, long steps wider (underlined in green) and rests dashed.
  - Knobs: RATE · GATE · OCTAVE (±2) · HOLD.

Sequencers follow the tempo, and they record onto the tape like anything else you play.

## 9. FX rack

Press **I** for the master rack. It has four effects in a chain:

**DRIVE → CHORUS → DELAY → REVERB**

- T1–T4 select an effect. Press the same T again to switch it on or off.
- **Shift + FX** (Shift + I) switches to **bank B**, a second chain of four effects; the FX key lights orange while bank B is on screen. Shift + FX again goes back to bank A. Effects in both banks can be on at the same time.
- The four lights on the FX key show which effects are on, in either bank.

| Effect | Knobs |
|---|---|
| **DRIVE** | DRIVE · TYPE (TAPE, TUBE, FUZZ) · TONE · MIX |
| **CHORUS** | RATE · DEPTH · WIDTH · MIX |
| **DELAY** | TIME (synced to tempo) · FEEDBK · AGE · MIX |
| **REVERB** | DECAY · TONE · SPRING (PLATE at zero) · MIX |

**Bank B** (Shift + FX), after bank A in the chain: **FILTER → GATE → FLANGER → LO-FI**

| Effect | Knobs |
|---|---|
| **FILTER** | FREQ (turn left for low-pass, right for high-pass, centre is open) · RESO · WOBBLE (sweeps with the beat) · MIX |
| **GATE** | RATE (1/4 to 1/32, synced to tempo) · DEPTH · SMOOTH · MIX |
| **FLANGER** | RATE · DEPTH · FEEDBK · MIX |
| **LO-FI** | CRUSH (12 down to 3 bits, with hiss) · TONE · WOW · MIX |

Delay and reverb tails ring out when you switch them off. Both banks are included in the WAV export.

### Live FX: hold 1–8

With the FX screen open, hold the number keys for performance effects on everything you hear: the tape, your playing and the drums. Each one works while you hold its key and stops when you let go.

| Key | Effect |
|---|---|
| **1–4** | **REPEAT** 1/4 · 1/8 · 1/16 · 1/32: the beat you are on plays once, then repeats in time with the tempo |
| **5** | **FREEZE**: holds the sound as a steady texture |
| **6** | **SWEEP DOWN**: a resonant low-pass closes over two beats |
| **7** | **SWEEP UP**: a high-pass rises over two beats, for build-ups |
| **8** | **TAPE STOP**: everything slows down to a stop |

You can hold several at once, such as a sweep with a repeat. Of the repeats, FREEZE and TAPE STOP, the last one you pressed wins. The bottom of the screen shows the eight effects with the ones you hold lit, and the effect's name appears big while you hold it. Live FX are for performing: they are in LIVE MIXDOWN and in resampling (sampler RECORD, MASTER), but they are not recorded on the tape.

## 10. Tempo, count-in and quantize

Press **TEMPO** (**`**) for the tempo screen.

| Knob | Normal | With Shift held |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | BPM (40–200) | SWING |
| Green | COUNT-IN (off, 1 bar, 2 bars) | BEATS per bar |
| White | QUANTIZE (off, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32) | SNAP on/off |
| Orange | Metronome CLICK level | METRONOME on/off |

- **Metronome:** hold Shift and turn the orange knob right to switch it on, left to switch it off (or push the knob). The TEMPO key blinks on every beat while it is on, and the beat dots on the tempo screen light up.
- **Count-in:** pressing REC from stop clicks you in, and the tape starts on the downbeat. Press STOP or REC to cancel.
- **Quantize:** notes from the keys or MIDI snap to the grid, which follows the tape's bars. Notes that are only slightly late play straight away. Sequencers also start on the grid.
- **Snap:** loop, lift and drop line up with bars and beats.

Click **connect midi** to use any connected MIDI keyboard. Supported:

- Note velocity
- Pitch bend
- Mod wheel, which sets the LFO amount
- Sustain pedal

WEB-1 can also drive and follow other gear. In tape mode, open the system menu (Shift + 8) and pick **MIDI**:

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| **CONNECT** | Asks the browser for MIDI, like the connect midi button, and shows how many inputs and outputs it found |

## 12. Looks: dark mode, OLED and line art

The buttons under the device change how WEB-1 looks (on OS-1 they are in the controls card: Shift + ?):

- **dark mode:** a graphite case and keys, with the same black screen. The black lines between the keys stay black. Switch it off for the light aluminium case with the black screen.
- **oled:** adds a subtle glow (halation) to the screen, like light spreading on an OLED panel.
- **line art:** draws the whole device as flat outlines: thin lines for the keys, knob bases, keyboard and case edge, solid circles for the black keys, coloured knob caps and a black screen. Together with **dark mode** it becomes light lines on a dark-grey case, still with a black screen.

All three settings are remembered, and **reset all** does not change them.

### The screens

Every picture is drawn to fill the screen, from edge to edge between the title and the four values. They share one style: lines with round ends, small labels in spaced capitals (VOICES, FILTER, DECAY, RESONANCE…), and any extra amount drawn as a labelled bar on a dark track, such as SUB, TONE, OCTAVE, SWELL, BRIGHT or DIVE.

- **Bank A (A1–A8):** CLUSTER (the voices beside the filter curve, with a ring on the resonance), FM (the modulator circling the carrier on a dashed orbit, beside the decay curve), PULSE (the wave with its width in %, and PWM and SUB labelled), ORGAN (four tall drawbars in rounded slots), FOLD (the folded wave with OCTAVE and TONE bars) and STORM each draw their sound large across the screen.
- **STRINGS (A3):** a vintage string-machine panel in flat lines. Its four sliders (TONE, PHASE, CELLO, VIB) follow the four knobs in their colours, the PHASER light pulses at the phaser's speed, and the level meter and two-octave keyboard light up as you play.
- **PLUCK:** six strings over a round sound hole. Every note that sounds shakes the string nearest its pitch, so chords shake several strings; a note you let go of stops shaking quickly. The rings round the hole follow BODY, and an orange mark shows where the strings are plucked.
- **TINE:** the tines and tone bars of an electric piano, one per key. A held note's tine rings and its hammer lights; two lights at the top swing from left to right with the tremolo.
- **BOOM:** a subwoofer that pushes out with each note, with the pitch drop drawn beside it and a dot that follows the last note.
- **MALLET:** a row of bars over resonator tubes, coloured by the material (wood orange, metal blue, glass white). A note lights its bar while it is held, or for a moment after a quick tap, and a mallet comes down on it, and the little fans in the tubes spin with the tremolo.
- **GRAIN:** the sound being cut up (the slot's sample or the built-in texture), with the window POSITION and SPRAY pick from, and each grain as a dot that rises and fades as it plays.
- **VOWEL:** a little choir of three singers. Their mouths take the shape of the vowel (A wide open, E wide, I a thin smile, O round, U small and pursed) and open wider as the sound gets louder. SIZE makes them small or tall, BREATH puffs from their mouths and VIBRATO makes them sway. The vowel is shown big on the left, and the three formant peaks stand as coloured bars on the right.
- **CHIP:** the wave in big pixels on the left, and a pixel piano roll on the right: each step of the arpeggio adds a block at its note's height, newest on the right (root blue, third green, fifth white, octave orange), and the newest block sparkles. Without an arpeggio, a held note draws a steady line of blocks; when nothing plays, a few pixels twinkle.
- **FELT:** inside a piano: a felt hammer strikes the strings when its note starts, the damper lifts while the key is held, and the strings ring down for a moment after you let go, and two little tape reels turn with WOBBLE.
- **HERO (A7):** an arcade space shooter. A pixel ship follows your pitch and glides with GLIDE, every note fires a laser, and aliens burst into pixels with a running score.
- **VELVET (B4):** a retro android in thin flat lines. Its LED visor lights up like an equaliser for your chord, its ear discs and the two speaker stacks pump with the level.
- **BRASS (B5):** a vintage polysynth in isometric line art. Its keys go down in orange as you play, its four panel knobs follow the four encoders, and a raised display traces the filter envelope, with a needle VU meter and A·D·S·R faders beside it.
- **NEON (B1):** a synthwave sunset in flat colour bands: a striped sun over a perspective grid that scrolls faster as you play.
- **AURORA (B6):** flat aurora curtains rippling at the ensemble speed, over stars and layered hills.
- **HOOVER (B8):** three thick saw waves in blue, green and white swirling over each other. At each note they bend up from a lower pitch (DIVE, also shown as a labelled bar), DETUNE pulls them apart, CHORUS makes them sway and the orange bar shows TONE.
- **LADDER, ORBIT, ACID:** live filter curve (with a dashed cutoff mark and its frequency in HZ), ringed planet (with a SWELL bar) and resonant peak (with its Q).
- **Sampler and pad sample editor:** both use the same screen: the waveform with START and END markers, fade ramps, a green loop mark, the play mode, and an orange playhead for each note or hit that is still sounding.
- **Effects (T3):** all eight effects draw their picture on the left with two big numbers on the right.
- **Tape:** two views, picked in SYSTEM → DISPLAY → TAPE VIEW, both using the whole screen with no knob values along the bottom. **TAPE FOCUS** has two big turning reels with the tape running over rollers and the head, the time, bar and section between them, the track number large in its colour with a hollow FX box that turns orange while effects are on, the speed gauge on the right edge, and one thin coloured line per track along the bottom. **TRACK FOCUS** has small reels and the time on top, a strip of the whole tape with its sections, and four tall lanes with each track's audio, level and MUTE, SOLO or REC. In both, hold Shift to see the four edits (COPY · CROP · DOUBLE · ERASE) and an outline around the part they will change; the name of the edit you just used flashes on screen.
- **Mixer:** channel strips with thick meters and pans below (T1), three compressor bands (T2), the real EQ curve with four dots (T3), and output and gain-reduction meters (T4).
- **Key & chords:** the key large in blue, the last chord in orange, a keyboard showing the notes that sound, and the chord on each step of the scale.
- **Menus:** the synths & presets menu shows the slot number large on the left with two scrolling lists; the CHAIN sequencer shows its step count large above the chain of steps.
- The NEON, AURORA, VELVET and BRASS screens use only the four knob colours, as flat lines and fills.

## 13. Saving and exporting

- Everything saves automatically in your browser: sounds, slot settings, sequences, the mixer, the FX rack, samples and the tape.
- Your work is kept as **projects** (Shift + 7 in tape mode), and you can save any of them, or all of them, to a **.web1 file** as a backup. See below.
- The first time you open WEB-1, you are in the **DEMO SONG** project. Start your own song with **NEW** in PROJECTS; the demo stays in its own project, and **DEMO SONG** in PROJECTS makes it again whenever you like.
- **export tape as wav** downloads the full tape mix, including the master FX.
- **reset all** erases every saved sound, sequence, sample and the tape. You are asked to confirm first.
- The **system menu** (Shift + 8 in tape mode) can erase just the samples, just your presets, just the tape, or everything, and shows how much space is used.
- **Offline:** once you have opened WEB-1, it opens again with no internet connection, and so does OS-1 from the home screen. When you are online, it loads the newest version.
- **USB stick (WEB-1 hardware):** with a stick plugged in, WAV exports, stems and .web1 files are saved into the WEB-1 folder on the stick instead of downloading. See [On the WEB-1 hardware](#16-on-the-web-1-hardware).

Your work lives in the browser you made it in. Clearing that site's data, or using a private window, starts fresh. Save a **.web1** backup of anything you want to keep, and export WAVs to use the audio elsewhere.

### Projects: Shift + 7

A project is one whole song: its tape, every sound and kit setting, the sequences, the drum machine, the mixer and FX, the key and chords, and the samples. In tape mode, hold **Shift** and press **7** (labelled **PROJECT**) to open **PROJECTS**. Key 7 lights orange. The tape screen's title shows the name of the open project.

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|

| **NEW** | Starts a new project (and keeps the one you were in): empty, or from a starter picked with the orange knob. A starter sets the tempo and swing, the key with scale lock on, a keys sound on A1 and a bass on A2, a drum machine beat (already running), a chord progression in CHAIN (tap the low C to play it) and FOLLOW BASS on A2. LO-FI BEAT: 82 BPM in D minor, soft keys and a swung boom-bap beat. HOUSE LOOP: 124 BPM in A minor, stabs, an offbeat bass and four on the floor. Your saved presets and the system settings (time, display) are shared by every project. The **save project** and **load project** buttons under the device save the open project and load a file in one click. A .web1 file keeps the audio at 16 bits and is compressed, so a short song is small; four full tracks of 60 seconds come to at most about 23 MB. Files made on a device with another sample rate are converted when you open them. **reset all** now erases every project.

### Undo: Shift + DROP

Hold **Shift** and press **DROP** (Shift + ]) to undo the last change, wherever you made it:

- **Sounds and settings:** a knob turn, a preset or engine change, a step or pattern edit, a mixer or FX move, the tempo, a scene. Each knob turn or edit is one step; touching another knob starts a new one.
- **The tape:** a recording (so a take in REPLACE mode can be taken back), LIFT, DROP, Shift + LIFT, CROP, DOUBLE or ERASE.

Press it again to go further back, up to 64 steps. **Shift + STOP** (Shift + Enter) is **REDO**: it steps forward again. A small label at the top of the screen says what changed, for example **UNDO · CUTOFF** or **REDO · TAPE**. While you hold Shift in tape mode, the top right of the tape screen shows **DROP ▸ UNDO** and what it will undo.

The tape keeps only its last change, so an older tape edit can't be undone once you make a new one. Erasing the whole tape (Shift + TAPE) can't be undone. Switching slot, kit, octave or mode, the system settings and your saved presets aren't part of undo.

## 14. Your first song in 10 steps

**Before you start:** if you are in the DEMO SONG project (the title bar of the tape screen shows the project's name), start a new one so you record on an empty tape: in tape mode press **Shift + 7**, turn the green knob to **NEW** and push the white knob (or turn it to YES). The demo stays in its own project.

1. Press **TEMPO** (**`**) and set the BPM with the blue knob. Turn the green knob to **1 BAR** count-in, then press TEMPO again to close the screen.
2. Press **X** for drums and pick a kit with **1**. Press **C** for tape and **B** to select track 1.
3. Press **R**. After the count-in, play a beat on the keys for a few bars, then press **Enter**.
4. Press **Enter** twice to rewind, then press **Q** to loop the first bars.
5. Press **N** to select track 2. Press **Z** for synth, then **Shift + 7** for ACID and play a bassline while recording with **R**. Overlap notes to make them slide.
6. Press **C** and select track 3 with **M**. Press **Z**, then **Shift + 6** for AURORA and record some long chords. If you shaped a sound you like, press **Shift + B** to save it as a preset, type a name and press **Enter**.
7. Press **C** and select track 4 with **,**. Press **Z**, then **Shift + 1** for NEON and record a lead.
8. Press **V** for the mixer and balance the four tracks with the knobs.
9. Press **I**, then switch on DELAY (T3) and REVERB (T4) for space.
10. Click **export tape as wav** to download your song.

**A shortcut for chords and bass:** in step 6, open KEY & CHORDS (Shift + `), press T2 and push MAKE for a progression, then press T3 and pick a slot for the bass. Tap the low C while recording, and the chords and a bass line go onto the track in one take.

## 15. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| A track is silent on playback | It may be muted (key 1–4 lit orange in mixer mode) or left out of a solo (keys 5–8). The title bar shows MUTE or SOLO. |
| All my tracks keep dipping | The sidechain is on: Shift + MIXER, T2, set SOURCE to OFF. |
| I recorded over something by mistake | Press **Shift + DROP** straight away to undo the take. |
| I want my work on another computer | Open PROJECTS (Shift + 7 in tape mode), choose **BACKUP ALL**, and load the file there with **load project**. |
| A key plays a different note, or a whole chord | KEY & CHORDS is on (the title bar shows it, for example A MIN · TRIAD). Open it with Shift + TEMPO and set SCALE and CHORD to OFF. |
| No sound | Click the device or press a key first, since browsers need a click before audio can play. Check the volume knob and the MIXER levels. |
| Keys don't play | Click the page so it has keyboard focus, and make sure no text field is selected. |
| Recording is silent | Check REC LVL (tape mode) and that the right track is selected. With count-in on, recording starts after the clicks. |
| The screen goes black after a while | SCREEN SLEEP is on in the system menu (Shift + 8 in tape mode, DISPLAY). Any key or knob wakes it. |
| The screen looks dim | Turn BRIGHTNESS up in the system menu (DISPLAY). |
| REC blinks but nothing records | ADVANCED REC (Shift + REC) is set to start ON SOUND or on the NEXT BAR. Play louder or lower the THRESHOLD, wait for the bar, or set START back to KEY. |
| Recording wipes what was already on the track | KEEP OLD in ADVANCED REC is below 100%. Turn it back up to overdub. |
| Recording stops by itself | AUTO STOP is on in ADVANCED REC. Set it to OFF. |
| Microphone or MIDI doesn't work | Serve the page from `localhost` or HTTPS, and use Chrome or Edge for MIDI. |
| The letter keys type instead of playing notes | The SAVE PRESET screen is open. Press **Esc** to close it, or **Enter** to save. |
| Crackles | Close other heavy tabs. Very big chords on NEON or HOOVER use the most CPU. The system menu's CPU section shows the audio load and dropouts. |
| Fewer notes sound than I play, and the title bar shows CPU | The device is busy, so the AUTO voice limit is holding back. Close other tabs, or set VOICE LIMIT in the system menu (CPU). |
| A bass plays along by itself | FOLLOW BASS is on (the title bar shows BASS A1, for example). Open KEY & CHORDS (Shift + `), press T3 and set SLOT to OFF. |
| Pressing T1 opened the sampler | Pressing T1 on the T1 page opens the SAMPLER. Press T1 on its EDIT page to go back. |
| Some steps only play now and then | They have a CHANCE below 100% or a condition (1 OF 2, FILL…). Hold the step, turn CHANCE up, or hold Shift and set COND to ALWAYS. |
| ← and → don't bend the pitch | The PATTERN sequencer is on screen: there the arrows pick the step. Close the sequencer screen to bend. |
| My recording plays on top of the demo song | You are in the DEMO SONG project, where all four tracks are already used. Start a new project: Shift + 7 in tape mode, then NEW. |
| The tour is waiting and won't go on | Each step waits for the key or knob it points at (it blinks orange). Use it, or press Shift + STOP to end the tour. |
| A tip covers the bottom of the screen | Tips go away after a few seconds, and each one only shows once. SYSTEM → DISPLAY → TIPS turns them off. |
| I want a fresh start | Click **reset all**. |

## 16. On the WEB-1 hardware

WEB-1 is also designed as a physical instrument: a Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 runs this same app on a 4.3" screen, with real keys, clicking knobs, a speaker and a battery. The [build guide](https://vatskyone.github.io/effective-barnacle/web1-3d.html) has the 3D model, the parts, the wiring and the software setup. Everything in this guide works the same on the device, with these extras:

- **It starts in your last project.** Switch it on and WEB-1 opens where you left off, with the sound ready and no click needed. The very first time, it makes the demo song and starts the guided tour, whose steps light up the keys to press.
- **The knobs click.** One click is one step, and pressing a knob pushes it (see [Knob clicks and pushes](#knob-clicks-and-pushes)).
- **Battery.** The title bar shows the charge, with ⚡ while charging. SYSTEM → BATTERY shows the level, the charging state and the cell voltage.
- **Turning off.** Choose SYSTEM → BATTERY → **POWER OFF** and push the white knob twice: WEB-1 saves everything, shows SAVED, and shuts down. Then slide the switch to OFF. The switch on its own cuts the power at once, so use POWER OFF first.
- **Clock and timezone.** TIME and TIMEZONE in the system menu set the device's own clock.
- **USB stick.** WAV exports, stems, SAVE FILE and BACKUP ALL go to the WEB-1 folder on the stick when one is in. PROJECTS → LOAD FILE lists the .web1 files on it, and SYSTEM → STORAGE → LOAD SAMPLE FROM USB loads a sample (WAV, MP3, AIFF or FLAC) into the synth slot you were on. Choose STORAGE → EJECT USB before you pull it out.
- **Line in.** The IN jack records like the microphone: set INPUT to LINE IN in advanced REC (Shift + REC, then Shift + orange knob).
- **Updates.** SYSTEM → ABOUT → CHECK FOR UPDATE, then INSTALL UPDATE (two pushes, needs Wi-Fi). WEB-1 restarts with the new version and keeps your work.
- **The CPU section** of the system menu shows how hard the device is working, and the AUTO voice limit keeps the sound clean when it is busy.
